Research Interests

The security of LLM systems that act on retrieved content: making RAG assistants and tool-using agents reliable when that content is wrong, and secure when an attacker wrote it.

  • Prompt injection and retrieval poisoning: how often untrusted content (scraped pages, retrieved documents, tool outputs and MCP tool descriptions, agent memory) changes what an agent does, and what defenses such as detection, isolation, and least-privilege tool access cost in task utility.
  • Multilingual robustness: whether prompt injection detectors and guardrails built mostly for English hold up on Bangla and code-mixed Banglish text.

These questions come from building Conversa AI, a production assistant that answers from scraped web content and takes actions such as lead capture and handover to a person.

Research Projects

Shotota: Semantic Plagiarism Detection for Bangla Text

Feb – Nov 2023

Senior Design Project (CSE 499A/B), North South University · Supervisor: Dr. Nabeel Mohammed

  • Built a plagiarism detection system for Bangla that finds reworded text through sentence-level semantic similarity instead of exact string matching: sentences are encoded with Bangla-BERT, stored in a vector database, and matched against the query document.
  • Designed the text preprocessing pipeline (removing punctuation, digits, stop words, extra spaces, and non-Bangla sentences) and compared four preprocessing variants by their effect on similarity scores for paraphrased sentences. Stemming gave the highest scores but was unreliable because Bangla stemming tools are limited, so punctuation removal without stemming was chosen as the safer default.
  • Embedded the Bangla2B+ corpus (27 GB, about 138M sentences) in 40 MB chunks of about 200,000 sentences, each taking about an hour on Colab. Embedding the full corpus would take an estimated 691 GPU-hours, so the prototype indexed a subset.
  • Compared Milvus (HNSW, IVF_FLAT) and Qdrant on insertion, indexing, and search time. Milvus was faster (about 3.3 s vs. 8.0 s to insert 1,000 vectors) but couldn't store Bangla text, so the system switched to Qdrant.
  • On hand-built paraphrase examples, similarity stayed above 0.90 for light rewording but fell to 0.60–0.80 for heavy paraphrasing, below the system's 0.80 plagiarism threshold, so heavy rewording can evade detection. Splitting one sentence into several shorter ones gave 0.57–0.85, so this kind of rewording is harder to catch at the sentence level.
  • My role (team of four): preprocessing, BERT embedding, and the Qdrant retrieval layer.

Parameter Averaging and Model Ensembling for Food Image Classification

Fall 2022

Course Project, Pattern Recognition and Neural Networks (CSE 465), North South University · Unpublished manuscript with M. K. Wasi, S. U. R. Shaikh, and R. H. Borshon

  • Compared weight averaging during training (averaging each epoch's weights with the best earlier checkpoint) and six ensembling methods on Food-11 using AlexNet, InceptionV3, ResNet50, and VGG16 — 74 experiments over 4 single models and all 11 combinations.
  • Weight averaging improved 3 of 4 single models (ResNet50 dropped slightly). The best result, 95.24%, came from output-averaging weight-averaged InceptionV3, ResNet50, and VGG16.
  • Ensembles built from weight-averaged models beat their regular counterparts in 8 of 11 combinations. Most differences were under one percentage point and came from single runs, so the result points in a direction but doesn't settle it.
  • My role (team of four): built the ResNet50 and VGG16 ensembles and the averaging comparisons.

Skin Cancer Detection using Lightweight Model Souping and Ensembling Knowledge Distillation for Memory-Constrained Devices

M. R. Kabir, R. H. Borshon, M. K. Wasi, R. M. Sultan, A. Hossain, R. Khan

JournalIntelligence-Based Medicine, vol. 10, 100176, Elsevier, 2024

  • Distilled ResNet50 and DenseNet161 teacher models (98.32% and 98.80% accuracy on HAM10000) into a 0.35M-parameter student, TinyStudent, with 73–82× fewer parameters. The single-teacher students reached 85.45% and 85.00%; distilling from both teachers at once gave 84.10%.
  • Compared output ensembling and greedy model soup over the three students. Concatenation ensembling reached 88.00% (about 1.05M parameters); greedy soup reached 85.70% while keeping a single 0.35M model at inference.
  • Deployed the student model in a Django web app to show it runs on low-memory devices.
  • My contribution (CRediT: methodology, validation, visualization): implemented the greedy model soup experiments. The soup kept only one of the three students, because adding the others lowered validation accuracy.
